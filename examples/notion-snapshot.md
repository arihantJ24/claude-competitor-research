## Assessment

**Strengths.** Growth at scale is unusual: ~$865M ARR growing 82% [3·est] with the company itself saying over half of ARR now comes from AI-enabled customers [5·disc]. The pricing architecture is doing real work — AI is the carrot that moves teams from $10 Plus to $20 Business, and credits add a usage layer on top [1·disc]. Distribution is global and bottom-up (100M+ users, ~80% outside the US [4·rep]), which is hard to replicate.

**Weaknesses.** Product focus is narrowing by subtraction: Mail killed 14 months after launch [6], Calendar unchanged since Feb-25 [3], Campsite bought but no chat product shipped [3,8]. That is a pattern of adjacencies being abandoned in favour of the agent bet. Public figures are mostly second-hand — Notion discloses valuation and ARR mix but not ARR, users or headcount, so headcount estimates span 1,000–4,800 [7] and every traction number above except the tender is reported or estimated.

**Threat level.** n/a (no baseline). Re-run as Battle Card with a baseline to get one.

**Watch.** (1) Whether credit-based pricing for Custom Agents/Workers becomes a meaningful second revenue line or a churn irritant — the pricing page already splits seat vs credits [1]. (2) The External Agents API (Claude Code, Codex) — if Notion becomes the data layer other agents act on, it competes with Confluence/SharePoint on a new axis [3]. (3) Any further product retirements after Mail; Calendar is the obvious candidate [3,6].

**So what for us.** If Notion is in your competitive set, the decision this quarter is whether to price AI as bundled-in-tier (Notion's $20 Business) or as metered credits — Notion is now doing both, and its pricing page is the clearest public benchmark for that hybrid. If Notion is a potential integration partner rather than a competitor, the May-26 Developer Platform (database sync + external agents) is the surface to evaluate.
